# design.md

## 1. Brand

Construction foremen hire SlipLess to reallocate their resources in response to labor turnover or equipment downtime. It should feel professional, bold, grounded.

## 2. Inspiration

Trend borrowed: cinematic editorial — use large, high-impact imagery, oversized typography, strong geometric layouts, and restrained industrial color accents to make SlipLess feel like a serious construction operations product rather than generic business software.

## 3. Color

Base hue from: Deep charcoal / warm black, with construction orange as the primary brand accent and muted steel-blue as a secondary environmental color.

Harmony: Complementary — the system pairs warm orange with cool blue/steel tones, while charcoal, white, and gray act as neutrals. The orange/blue relationship is visible across the two references, while the dark/light neutrals provide most of the interface structure.

Avoid-list check:

SlipLess direction: charcoal + construction orange + off-white, with restrained steel-blue imagery as a secondary color. This keeps the palette tied to the references without falling into common AI-generated SaaS color patterns.

## 4. Typography

Display: Bold geometric sans-serif, likely Montserrat-style. Heavy weight, uppercase, wide proportions, tight-to-moderate tracking. The headline uses a large geometric grotesk rather than a decorative display face.

Body: Sans-serif, likely a neutral geometric/grotesque. Regular weight with relatively compact sizing and generous enough spacing for navigation and supporting copy.

Mono: None needed. The reference does not use a monospace treatment, and SlipLess does not need one unless technical metrics or operational data are being emphasized.

Why this pair: A heavy geometric sans gives SlipLess the same direct, industrial presence as the reference, while a more neutral sans for supporting text keeps operational information readable and practical.

Closest free Google Fonts:

Display:

Montserrat — closest overall. Compare the uppercase A, R, and M; Montserrat's geometric construction is particularly close to the reference.

League Spartan — more condensed and forceful. Compare the G and overall width of DREAM.

Poppins — more circular and geometric. Compare the O, R, and curved portions of S.

Body:

Inter — neutral and highly readable. Compare the lowercase a, e, and numerals in navigation/supporting text.

DM Sans — slightly softer and more geometric. Compare the a, t, and e.

Manrope — more contemporary/geometric. Compare the a, g, and overall roundness of lowercase text.

Scale: for a 1440px desktop frame:

H1: 72–80px

H2: 40–48px

H3: 28–32px

Body: 16–18px

Small labels / navigation: 12–14px

Button text: 12–14px

Headlines: Use a 700–800 weight for H1/H2. The reference headline is uppercase with tight line spacing and relatively tight tracking. A SlipLess H1 should use approximately 0 to -1px letter spacing and a line height around 0.9–1.0em.

Two-tone headline: Yes. The reference headline is effectively two-tone through white type over the darkened image, with the visual emphasis coming from the image/overlay rather than alternating text colors. For SlipLess, preserve the principle with a primary light headline and optionally use the brand accent color on one short word or phrase, rather than coloring entire lines differently.

## 5. Logo

Type: wordmark — "SLIPLESS" in bold Montserrat

Why: the short, distinctive brand name works on its own without a separate symbol. The bold geometric wordmark is highly legible, feels practical and modern, and keeps the identity simple across the website, product, and construction-site contexts.

Files: assets/logo-full.svg, assets/logo-mark.svg, assets/favicon.svg

Clear space: one letter-height around the wordmark on all sides

Minimum size: 24px mark, 120px wordmark

Never: add a separate G-shaped symbol, recolor beyond #151218, #FFFDFC, and #F86D26, stretch, rotate, add effects, alter letter spacing, or place on a photo without a solid panel

## 6. Layout

Grid: 12 columns, max content width 1200px, 24px gutters, with 32–48px side padding on desktop. The layouts consistently align headlines, imagery, cards, and navigation to shared vertical edges rather than positioning each element independently.

Spacing scale: 8px base unit.

8px — label-to-heading and small internal gaps

16px — icon/text and small component gaps

24px — card padding and related content groups

32px — navigation, CTA, and section-level gaps

48px — major content separation

64px — hero/content separation

96px — section-to-section spacing

128px — large hero or page-level spacing

Radius: 0–4px for primary content blocks. The references generally use squared-off construction imagery and cards; rounded treatment is reserved for small UI elements such as Shot 2's navigation and CTA.

Shadow: Minimal or no visible shadow on major sections. Where elevation is needed, use a soft, low-contrast shadow rather than a pronounced floating-card effect.

Gestalt principles:

Proximity: Elements that belong together are kept tightly grouped. In Shot 1, the eyebrow, headline, description, and CTAs form one compact hero block; the three feature cards similarly group image, title, and supporting content.

Similarity: Repeated orange accents identify interactive or emphasized elements across the page. Shot 1 repeats orange in the eyebrow, headline accent, and CTAs; Shot 3 repeats orange across the logo, backgrounds, and product graphics.

Common region: Sections are separated into distinct visual regions. Shot 1 uses the dark hero as one region and the white content area as another; Shot 3 uses large orange/white blocks to establish separate regions.

Focal point: Large imagery and oversized typography establish an obvious first point of attention. Shot 2 places the construction scene behind the large "MAKE YOUR DREAM" headline, while Shot 1 pairs the headline directly with overlapping building imagery.

Continuity: Strong horizontal and vertical alignments guide the eye through the page. Navigation, headline blocks, imagery, and cards repeatedly share common left/right edges.

Rule 1: Keep primary content inside a 1200px max-width 12-column grid, and align major headlines, imagery, and CTAs to the same column boundaries.

Rule 2: Use 8px-based spacing, with 24–32px for related elements and 64–96px between major sections.

Rule 3: Establish one dominant focal point per viewport—a major headline or operational visual—and keep secondary elements from competing at the same scale.

Rule 4: Group related information with proximity and common region: if two elements describe the same action or concept, keep them within 24–32px and avoid unnecessary containers or dividers.

## 7. Imagery

Photos: real construction sites, crews, equipment, materials, and active job sites. Use documentary-style photography with strong natural or directional light, high contrast, and visible construction context. Prefer full-bleed or large rectangular crops. Show people doing the work rather than posed portraits. Use orange safety gear and machinery as natural accent colors.

Crops: use bold architectural angles, low viewpoints, close working details, and wide site shots. Avoid overly polished corporate stock photography.

3D / illustration: limited 3D only when showing SlipLess product concepts, equipment, or operational objects. Keep renders realistic and industrial. No abstract blobs, cartoon illustrations, or decorative 3D.

Icons: simple geometric SVG icons using solid shapes or 2px stroke. Use the orange accent for active states and white or charcoal for neutral states. Icons should communicate a concrete construction or operational concept rather than decorate empty space.


## 8. Voice

Three words: practical, direct, confident

Headline formula: [action / operational problem] + [clear outcome]

Examples: "Reallocate Fast. Keep Crews Moving." / "When Labor Changes. Stay on Schedule." / "Equipment Down. Resources Moving."

Use short, concrete language that a foreman can understand immediately. Lead with the operational problem or action, then state the result. Avoid marketing language that does not describe a real outcome.


## 9. Do/Don't

Do: lead with a specific construction problem, action, or measurable outcome

Do: use real construction photography with people, equipment, buildings, and active job-site context

Do: use orange as a deliberate accent for CTAs, labels, key words, and active states

Do: keep headlines large, short, and easy to scan in one or two lines

Do: create strong contrast between dark surfaces, white content areas, and the orange accent

Don't: use generic corporate stock photos of handshakes, offices, or posed business teams

Don't: use gradients, neon effects, glows, or decorative effects that compete with the content

Don't: use vague headlines such as "Transform Your Business" or "Build a Better Future"

Don't: use orange for large amounts of body text or make every element an accent

Don't: mix unrelated illustration styles, icon styles, or decorative 3D elements on the same page
